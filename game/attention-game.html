The Economics of Attention

Information abundance shifts scarcity from information to efficient discrimination. Adaptive intelligence is efficient preservation of consequential distinctions.

Environment → expert compression → discarded residual → regime change → scout detection → adjudication → updated expertise.

Attention is a partition

A finite observer selects a support from a larger environment and compresses it. What lies outside that support is operationally invisible. Compression is efficient in a stable regime, but a discarded distinction can become consequential after a regime change.

The economic constraint

Exploration is not free. It creates candidate abundance; adjudication, false positives, delay, and switching create costs. Heterogeneous attention is valuable when the expected action value of residual discovery exceeds exploration plus adjudication cost.

The synthetic evidence ladder

The instruments progress from declared support sets, to costed adjudication, to a learned synthetic classifier, to bounded textual contexts and natural-language claims. The strongest semantic receipt uses GPT-5.6 Luna through Hermes Codex, but remains a constructed synthetic protocol with a residual token-cost confound. Nothing here establishes effects in humans, markets, institutions, or production AI.

What to retain

Expertise buys cheap exploitation and can create structured blindness. Scouts preserve non-redundant views. Adjudication decides whether a residual deserves recompression. The paper's claim is not “more diversity is always better.” It is that attention should pay for distinctions in proportion to their expected consequence.
