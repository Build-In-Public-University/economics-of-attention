# Argument map

1. Information can be abundant without becoming action-relevant.
   Depends on: Shannon distinction; finite observer boundary.

2. Finite observers require selective attention and lossy compression.
   Depends on: 1. Falsifier: no overload or filtering effect under fixed capacity.

3. Meaning is action-relative value added by preserving a distinction.
   Depends on: 2. Falsifier: surprise predicts consequential action as well as action-relative measures.

4. Attention mechanisms survive when expected action improvement exceeds sensing, processing, storage, interpretation, delay, and switching costs.
   Depends on: 3. Falsifier: persistent mechanisms do not show positive net value.

5. Markets are distributed observers whose prices compress dispersed signals while leaving residual information.
   Depends on: 2, 4, and verified Hayek/Grossman–Stiglitz sources.

6. Alpha is the temporary private value of a consequential distinction before socialization.
   Depends on: 3, 4, 5. Falsifier: profitable signals do not decay as attention diffuses.

7. Transformer attention is a structural computational analogue of query-conditioned selective weighting, not an identity claim about markets or minds.
   Depends on: 2, 3. Falsifier: the analogy predicts no useful routing or representation behavior.

8. Heterogeneous attention can preserve residual information under regime change, if adjudication permits minority observations to survive.
   Depends on: 2, 5, 7. Falsifier: heterogeneity provides no novelty-detection advantage under controlled regime shifts.

9. Language transmits reusable attention priors; semantic hysteresis makes historically invested filters slow to update.
   Depends on: 2, 3, 4. Falsifier: labels and historical investment do not alter feature weighting or update speed.

10. Adaptive intelligence is efficient preservation of consequential distinctions with enough exploratory diversity to notice when compression fails.
    Depends on: 1–9. This is the synthesis, not an empirical result.
