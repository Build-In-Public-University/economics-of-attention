# Foundational citation verification

Verification date: 2026-10-15

These sources were retrieved and read before updating the ledger. The citations establish the narrower bridges stated here; they do not validate the paper's synthesis.

## Shannon (1948)

Source: C. E. Shannon, “A Mathematical Theory of Communication,” Bell System Technical Journal 27 (1948), pp. 379–423, 623–656. Retrieved copy: Yale-hosted PDF, `cs.yale.edu/homes/lans/readings/general/shannon1948.pdf`.

Verified passage: Shannon states that messages frequently have meaning, but that “these semantic aspects of communication are irrelevant to the engineering problem.” He defines the engineering problem as reproducing a selected message and develops a logarithmic measure over possible messages.

Safe bridge: Shannon deliberately separates engineering transmission from semantic interpretation. The paper's action-relative meaning is an extension proposed here, not a quantity Shannon defines.

## Hayek (1945)

Source: F. A. Hayek, “The Use of Knowledge in Society,” American Economic Review 35(4) (1945), pp. 519–530. Retrieved copy: Yale-hosted PDF, `german.yale.edu/sites/default/files/hayek_-_the_use_of_knowledge_in_society.pdf`.

Verified passage: Hayek describes relevant knowledge as dispersed among individuals and argues that prices can coordinate separate actions. He emphasizes the “economy of knowledge” achieved when only essential information is passed to those concerned.

Safe bridge: Price can be analyzed as a compressed coordination signal for dispersed knowledge. “Lossy compression” is this paper's formal analogy, not Hayek's terminology.

## Grossman–Stiglitz (1980)

Source: Sanford J. Grossman and Joseph E. Stiglitz, “On the Impossibility of Informationally Efficient Markets,” American Economic Review 70(3) (1980), pp. 393–408. Retrieved copy: American Economic Association PDF, `aeaweb.org/aer/top20/70.3.393-408.pdf`.

Verified passage: The paper states that when arbitrage/information acquisition is costly, prices reflect informed traders' information only partially so informed traders can receive compensation. It models information acquisition as costly and the proportion of informed traders as endogenous.

Safe bridge: Costly information and partial price incorporation support the paper's residual-information mechanism. The paper's “alpha as unsocialized distinction” is a proposed interpretation, not a term used by Grossman–Stiglitz.

## Vaswani et al. (2017)

Source: Ashish Vaswani et al., “Attention Is All You Need,” arXiv:1706.03762, version 5; also NeurIPS 2017 proceedings.

Verified passage: The paper defines attention as mapping a query and key-value pairs to an output, with weights computed by query-key compatibility. It gives scaled dot-product attention as `softmax(QK^T / sqrt(d_k)) V` and describes multi-head attention as parallel projections into different representation subspaces.

Safe bridge: Transformer attention is a computational example of query-conditioned relational weighting. The market/attention analogy is structural and explicitly non-identity.
